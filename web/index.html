How it works

In deep sleep your brain makes big, slow waves, about one a second, and each one comes with a pulse of brain fluid (CSF) washing through. In September 2026 an MIT team (Levitt, Lewis et al., Science Translational Medicine) played 50 ms bursts of pink noise to sleeping people, and the bursts made those slow waves, and the fluid flow that follows them, stronger.

What Slowwave plays

  • The same sound: short, soft 50 ms bursts of pink noise.
  • Only after you've fallen asleep, in the first hours of the night, when most deep sleep happens.
  • Rhythm: groups of 5 bursts at slow-wave pace (about 0.8 per second). Pairs: 2 bursts, then a long rest.
  • Then silence for the rest of the night, so the dream-heavy morning sleep is left alone.
  • Nap: the same in short, with a gentle alarm at the end.

Getting the most out of it

  • Keep the bursts quiet: just loud enough to hear while you lie awake. If one ever wakes you, turn it down.
  • Rate your mornings. With Blind test on, some nights are silent and you only find out after rating, so you can see what the bursts really do for you.

Later

There's a hook for an EEG headband, which could fire each burst right on the peak of a slow wave, like in the study.
